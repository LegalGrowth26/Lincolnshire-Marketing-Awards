Awards Night Runbook

How the shortlist becomes a seating plan without you chasing anybody by hand. Tickets, guest names, dietary requirements and the whole email sequence, running off one dashboard.

Event 10 September 2026 Venue DoubleTree by Hilton, Lincoln Stack Next.js, Supabase, Stripe, Resend

The journey, end to end

Six states. A business moves through them on its own, and the dashboard shows you exactly where everyone is at any moment.

01 Shortlist imported You

Upload the judging CSV, match your columns, check the per category counts. Tick who is genuinely on the shortlist. Scores and results land in a separate table nothing else can read.

02 Invites armed You

Select the shortlisted rows and press Arm invites. Nothing has sent yet. This is the deliberate gap between importing and emailing, so a bad import can never reach anyone.

03 Congratulations sent Automatic

The daily job emails everyone armed: you have been shortlisted in this category, here is the evening, here are the two ticket links. No obligation to come, stated plainly.

04 Ticket bought Them

Stripe takes the payment and hands them straight back to a private booking page on your site. The order is recorded with a seat row per seat, one for a single, eight for a table.

05 Guests registered Them

They name each guest and tick dietary requirements per person, with free text for allergies and access needs. It saves as they go, so a table of eight does not have to be filled in one sitting.

06 Venue briefed You

Live dietary counts on the dashboard, plus a CSV for the venue with names and requirements only. A week out, everyone who booked gets the running order automatically.

The email sequence

One job runs at 9am daily and works out what is due. Everything is deduplicated, so a repeat run sends nothing twice. The master switch in Settings stops all of it.

Results stay in the room

Your spreadsheet holds scores, the winner and the highly commended. The build treats those as radioactive, and it is structural rather than a promise.

  • Scores and placement live in their own table that no public page, booking page or email can read.
  • Every email template declares the exact fields it accepts. Handing it a score throws an error instead of sending.
  • The shortlist view the dashboard uses does not join to results at all.
  • The venue CSV carries names and dietary needs. No category, no score, no placement.
  • On the shortlist screen, results sit behind a toggle that is off every time the page loads.
  • Shortlist size is never assumed. Four in most categories, two in one, six in another, all handled as data.

Getting it live

In order. Nothing can email anyone until the last step, so it is safe to work through this on the live site.

  1. Merge the code and deploy a preview Claude Code Paste the handoff prompt. It installs the packages, reconciles the new files, builds, and pushes a branch.
  2. Run the SQL migration You Supabase SQL Editor, paste the file, run. Creates everything and seeds the 15 categories.
  3. Set the environment variables You Fifteen values in Vercel. The full table is in SETUP.md.
  4. Point both Stripe links back at the site You After the payment, redirect to /api/stripe/return?session_id={CHECKOUT_SESSION_ID}. Turn on collect customer name while you are there.
  5. Add the Stripe webhook You Two events, then copy the signing secret into Vercel. This is what records the sale.
  6. Verify your sending domain in Resend You DNS records at your registrar. Send from your own domain or the invites land in spam.
  7. Import, test, then arm You Import the CSV, buy a test ticket in Stripe test mode, check the booking link works. Then arm the invites and switch automation on.
